Our team is made up of researchers, technologists, educators, and cultural practitioners, bringing deep experience in Hawaiian education, Indigenous technology development, Indigenous data sovereignty, AI for "low-resource" languages, and culturally-revitalizing educational technology.
 
Josiah Hester;
Josiah is Executive Director of Ulu Lāhui Foundation and Associate Professor of Interactive Computing at the Georgia Institute of Technology. As a Kanaka Maoli computer scientist, he founded the Ka Moamoa Ubiquitous Computing Lab to advance Indigenous communities through sustainable energy harvesting technologies, conservation, health wearables, interactive devices, and education that is culturally empowering. Through his work, he advocates for Hawaiian sovereignty, education, and environmental protection.

Rebecca Diego;
Rebecca is Director of Education & Programs at Ulu Lāhui Foundation. She has been a licensed active teacher in Hawaiʻi for over ten years, especially in STEM and Computer Science focused areas. She worked in Hawaiian language immersion public schools for most of her career, requiring delivery of course material that is relevant, place-based, and Hawaiian. She has led the design and implementation of culturally-based CS curricula that reflects the diverse backgrounds and experiences of students in grades K-12, to enhance student engagement, learning outcomes, and equity in access to STEM education.

Consultants:
Rachel Baker-Ramos; UX Design & Research Consultant.
Rachel is a research scientist with the Ka Moamoa Lab at the Georgia Institute of Technology, where she leads the Education and Public Interest HCI subgroup. With a background in experience design and qualitative research and 7+ years co-designing with communities, she has directed research & design for community-based, multi-partner projects, including conservation data dashboards, cultural-revitalizing educational games, and place-based bilingual ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi/English educational technology in Hawaiʻi.
 
Evyn-Bree Kalikolaukehaikaukaokukuiʻula Helekahi-Kaiwi; Cultural Transposition Consultant.
Evyn-Bree is a graduate of Hawaiian-medium education and a 2nd generation ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi speaker, whose family for four generations has committed actively to the revitalization of Hawaiian language in her family, and her community, Hāna. She has deep roots in Hāna, Maui and was immersed deeply in ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi (Hawaiian language), ʻike kuʻuna (ancestral knowledge), and the moʻomeheu (culture). She incorporates Hawaiian culture and values into her work, creating equitable and accessible learning experiences for ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi-speaking students.
 
Alakaʻi Iaea-Russell; Curriculum Design Consultant. 
Alakaʻi a is a Hawaiian-medium education and Ka Haka ʻUla o Keʻelikōlani graduate. As an educator, she works to combine culture, language, and education. She is passionate about revitalizing the Hawaiian language and helping keiki (children) connect to their community through place-based learning.
